01

EARLY LIFE & EDUCATION

From Rameswaram to a life of science.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, a town in Tamil Nadu. He grew up in a modest family and experienced a simple childhood. Despite limited resources, he developed a strong interest in learning and science.

During his school years, Kalam developed an interest in mathematics and physics. His determination to continue his education eventually took him to the Madras Institute of Technology, where he studied aeronautical engineering.

His education shaped the direction of his career. Rather than choosing an ordinary professional path, he wanted to contribute to India's scientific and technological development.

02

THE SCIENTIST

Building India's scientific capabilities.

After completing his education, Kalam joined India's scientific establishment. He worked with the Defence Research and Development Organisation and later became closely associated with the Indian Space Research Organisation.

At ISRO, he contributed to India's space programme and was associated with the Satellite Launch Vehicle-3 programme. The successful launch of the Rohini satellite in 1980 represented an important milestone in India's indigenous space capabilities.

Kalam later became one of the leading figures in India's integrated missile development efforts. His leadership and involvement in programmes such as Prithvi and Agni contributed to his popular identity as the "Missile Man of India."

04

THE PEOPLE'S PRESIDENT

A scientist who served the nation.

In 2002, Abdul Kalam became the 11th President of India. His election was significant because he came from a scientific and academic background rather than a traditional political career.

During his presidency, he became particularly popular among students and young people. His speeches often focused on education, scientific thinking, innovation, national development and the responsibility of young citizens.

His approachable personality and willingness to meet students earned him the affectionate title "People's President."

07

LEGACY

His greatest contribution was inspiration.

Kalam's legacy cannot be limited to his scientific achievements or his five years as President. His lasting influence came from the way he connected science with education, national development and the aspirations of young people.

He regularly interacted with students and encouraged them to ask questions, develop knowledge and take responsibility for India's future. Even after leaving the presidency, teaching and interacting with students remained central to his life.

For millions of Indians, Kalam represents the idea that a person can come from humble beginnings, pursue knowledge with determination and eventually contribute to the country at the highest level.
